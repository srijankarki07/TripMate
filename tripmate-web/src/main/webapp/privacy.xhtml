Privacy policy

Applies to the TripMate application at this address. Last updated 2 October 2026.

1. What TripMate is

TripMate is an application for planning a trip with a small group of people: a shared itinerary, a record of what everybody spent, and a running answer to who owes whom. It is operated as a student project, not by a company, and it is not a commercial service.

2. What is collected

When you create an account

  • Your name, your email address and, if you give one, your phone number.
  • Your password, stored only as a PBKDF2-SHA256 digest with a per-account random salt. The password itself is never written anywhere and cannot be recovered from what is stored.

When you use a trip

  • The trips you belong to and your role on each, the itinerary items, the expenses and how they were split, repayments recorded between members, and the summaries that have been emailed.
  • The times at which you do these things, which is what lets the application show a trip's history in order.

When you connect Google Calendar

This is optional and the application works without it. If you choose to connect it, you are asked for permission by Google and may decline. Section 4 describes exactly what is then received and done.

3. What it is used for

To run the application you asked for: to show you your trips and theirs to the people on them, to work out what each person owes, to send the emails the application sends, and to keep your calendar in step if you connected one. Nothing is used for advertising, nothing is sold, and there is no analytics or tracking of any kind.

4. Google Calendar

TripMate asks for one permission on your calendar, together with the basic information needed to know which Google account it is talking to:

  • Calendar events (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events), which is what lets it put your trips into a calendar.
  • Your basic profile and email address (openid, email), which is what lets it show you which account you connected, rather than leaving you to remember.

What TripMate does with it

  • It writes the itinerary items of the trips you belong to into your Google Calendar as events, and adds the trip's time zone so the times are right.
  • When an item is edited, it updates the event it created earlier rather than adding a second one. When an item is deleted, it deletes the event.
  • It records which Google account you connected, so it can show you.

What TripMate does not do with it

  • It does not read the events already in your calendar. The permission is used to create, update and delete the events this application itself created, and for nothing else.
  • It does not write anything anywhere except the calendar of the account that granted it. In particular, nothing is ever written into another member's calendar on your behalf unless they connected their own.
  • It does not share your calendar data with anybody, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to decide anything about you.
  • It does not transfer it to anyone else, except as needed to provide the feature itself, to comply with the law, or as part of a merger or acquisition if there ever were one.

TripMate's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What is stored, and for how long

To keep the connection working after you leave the page, Google gives the application a refresh token. That value is stored in the application's database encrypted, using AES-GCM with a key held in the server's own configuration rather than in the database, so a copy of the database alone does not contain a working credential. The short-lived access token is used and discarded and is never stored.

The refresh token is kept for as long as the connection exists and no longer. Disconnecting deletes it, and the application also asks Google to revoke the grant, which makes any copy of it useless.

How to withdraw it

  • In the application: open the trip, choose Send to a calendar, and press Disconnect. This deletes the stored token and tells Google to revoke it.
  • At Google: remove TripMate's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. This works even if you have stopped using TripMate.

Events already written into your calendar are yours and stay there after disconnecting. They are ordinary events and you can delete them in Google Calendar like any other.

5. Cookies and sessions

One cookie, containing a session identifier, is set when you sign in. It is what keeps you signed in, it is marked HttpOnly so that no script can read it, and it is deleted when you sign out. There are no advertising, analytics or third-party cookies, and the application does not embed anything from another site.

6. Who else sees any of it

The people on your trips see your name, the itinerary, the expenses and the balances. That is the point of the application. Beyond them:

  • Your email provider and Google, in the course of sending you an email or of the calendar connection you asked for.
  • Nobody else. There is no analytics provider, no advertising network, no error-tracking service and no data broker. Nothing is sold or rented, and no data is used to train anything.

7. Where it is kept

On the server this application runs on, in a PostgreSQL database. That server belongs to whoever is operating this deployment of TripMate. Data is not transferred to another country by the application itself.

8. Deleting your data

You can ask for your account and its data to be deleted at any time by writing to the address in section 11. Deletion is carried out within 30 days, and it removes:

  • your account, your name, your email address and your phone number;
  • your password digest and any outstanding password-reset links;
  • your membership of every trip, and your calendar connection and its stored token.

It does not remove the trips themselves. A trip belongs to everybody on it, and deleting your account would otherwise destroy records other people are relying on to settle up. Your name is removed from the expenses you paid, which stay in the trip without you.

Before you ask, it is worth disconnecting Google Calendar in the application if you connected it, since that withdraws the permission at Google as well as deleting the stored token.

9. Security

Passwords are stored as digests, never in readable form. Calendar refresh tokens are encrypted at rest. Sign-in issues a new session identifier, and signing out invalidates the session on the server. Traffic should be served over HTTPS in any deployment that is reachable from the internet.

No application can promise to be perfectly secure, and this one is a student project. It is built to hold trip plans and shared expenses, and it is a poor place for anything more sensitive than that.

10. Children

TripMate is not intended for children under 13, and accounts are not knowingly created for them.

11. Contact

Questions about this policy, and requests to delete your data, go to privacy@tripmate.example.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. If a change affects what is done with your Google data, you will be asked to reconnect, which means seeing and agreeing to the new permission rather than having it changed underneath you.
